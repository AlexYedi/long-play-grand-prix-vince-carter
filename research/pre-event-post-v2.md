## Step 3: Pre-Event LinkedIn Post

### Variant A: straight dispatch (2,613 / 3,000)

Four strangers, nine hours, and a problem venture has lived with for decades: LPs commit capital for ten years and get PDFs in return.

Today is Deal Flow, a VC hackathon at Betaworks hosted by localhost:nyc and VCLab with Tracy Huang. Teams of four, four tracks: sourcing, diligence, portfolio ops, LP reporting. Teams are set. We start building shortly.

5 places AI can earn its keep inside a venture firm:

1️⃣ Sourcing: ranking founders rather than finding more of them, with the reasoning and the warm intro path shown.

2️⃣ Diligence: first-meeting memos where every claim has a source. Probably the most crowded idea in the building, so sourcing discipline is what separates entries.

3️⃣ Portfolio monitoring: reading 40 updates in 40 formats and flagging runway trouble before the bridge request.

4️⃣ LP reporting: numbers an LP can check instead of a PDF they have to trust.

5️⃣ Firm memory: past memos, partner notes and relationship history. Context no data vendor can sell you.

We're building #4. The organizers put it plainly: "Valuation marks move with little explanation." At an emerging fund, the GP writes the quarterly letter by hand from founder emails in every format imaginable, and the LP can't check any of it.

Our read: for a Fund I manager, the scarce resource isn't hours. It's LP trust. 79% of LPs declined to re-up with at least one GP last year (Coller Barometer, across private markets), and Funds I–IV drew roughly 12% of H1 2025 commitments. A new manager has no track record to point to, so provable numbers have to stand in for one.

So: messy founder updates go in, and out comes a GP letter where every number traces to the founder's own sentence and every change from last quarter is explained. The GP decides what the LP sees. Nothing gets smoothed over silently. The question we're testing today: does provenance actually move an LP, or only the GP's workload?

5 things I'm leading with so four strangers are a team by 10:30:

→ Stack cards first. Each of us hands our AI a file describing our tools, and it maps the overlap, the gaps and who owns what before anyone writes code.

→ A 10-minute skills round: what you've shipped, what you're fastest in, when you have to leave.

→ Write the 3-minute demo script first, then build only what's on screen.

→ An ugly end-to-end version by lunch, then make it good.

→ "Blocked" gets said within 15 minutes, and everyone presents part of the demo.

If you've been on either side of an LP update: which number do you most wish you could click through to its source?

### Variant B: hiring-manager / commercial judgment (2,520 / 3,000)

The tools that trace portfolio numbers today mostly serve the GP. We're spending nine hours on the version that serves the LP.

Today is Deal Flow, a VC hackathon at Betaworks hosted by localhost:nyc and VCLab with Tracy Huang: teams of four across sourcing, diligence, portfolio ops and LP reporting.

Before picking a lane, I scored where AI has a buyer inside a venture firm:

1️⃣ Sourcing: founder ranking with visible reasoning and the warm intro path. Real pain, but every fund thinks its taste is the moat.

2️⃣ Diligence: sourced first-meeting memos. Valuable and crowded.

3️⃣ Portfolio monitoring: 40 updates in 40 formats, with runway trouble flagged before the bridge ask.

4️⃣ LP reporting: numbers an LP can verify instead of trust.

5️⃣ Firm memory: memos, partner notes, relationship history. Hard to sell, hard to copy.

We picked #4, and the commercial logic is the point. Collecting portfolio data is a busy market (Carta, Visible, Standard Metrics). The quarterly narrative, what moved and why, is still written by hand at most small funds. The organizers' own words: "Valuation marks move with little explanation."

The buyer is the emerging-manager GP, and the job isn't saving hours. It's earning trust. 79% of LPs declined to re-up with at least one GP last year (Coller Barometer, across private markets), and Funds I–IV drew roughly 12% of H1 2025 commitments. A Fund I can't show a long track record, but it can show numbers that trace back to the founder's own sentence, with every change from last quarter explained.

Our working shape: messy founder updates in, a source-linked GP letter out, and the GP decides what the LP sees. It sits next to the fund admin's report rather than replacing it. If it works, the LP view becomes the distribution: an LP who sees it asks their other GPs for it. The open question for today is whether provenance moves an LP decision, or only a GP's workload.

How I'm running a team of four people I met this morning:

→ Stack cards before code. Everyone's AI reads everyone's tool list and proposes who owns what.

→ Owners, not helpers: each seat has decision rights in its lane, and I own scope.

→ Demo script first. If it isn't on screen in three minutes, we don't build it.

→ End to end by lunch, ugly is fine.

→ Quality is settled by the scorer against a do-nothing baseline, not by opinion.

GPs and LPs: what would make a source-linked letter worth paying for, or worth asking your other GPs for?
