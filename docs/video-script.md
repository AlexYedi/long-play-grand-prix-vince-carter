[Card 1: the problem]
LPs commit capital for ten years and get PDFs they can't check. Washington State's pension fund publishes GP-reported returns with a footnote: they have not been confirmed by the general partners. For an emerging manager, that's the whole game. Seventy-nine percent of LPs have declined to re-up with at least one GP, and the quarterly reports a Fund One sends become the diligence file for Fund Two. Track Four names the problems: PDFs instead of data, reports in different formats, marks that move without explanation, and track records rebuilt by hand.

[Demo: review page → source → claims → approval → LP letter → real funds]
This is a synthetic fund: twenty-one messy founder updates from twenty-two companies. An extractor reads each one through Nicrron, and a number survives only if its quote appears word for word in the founder's text. Gantrell's email is upbeat. Underneath: revenue down thirteen percent, about four months of runway in a footnote, and a mark held flat. Click any number and you land on the founder's own sentence. "Biggest pipeline yet" is checked against the CRM, which can't confirm it. The GP can't approve until every red flag has a note, and only approved companies reach the LP letter. The ledger and every question to a founder are versioned in Roundtable. Then the market, with real data from Akta: March Capital and Left Lane Capital, each portfolio company against the competitors Akta lists. Pearl ranks second of five on launch velocity, but announced no new customers in the last ninety days. Every call is traced in PRISM, and extraction is right on nearly ninety-nine percent of fields, against eighteen percent for doing nothing.

[Card 2: approach, distribution, business]
Why this approach: a fluent AI letter makes a stale mark more believable, so we never trust the model. Code checks every number, and people approve only the exceptions. And it's headless: an MCP server any agent can query, tailored to the company, the GP and the LP. How it grows: a fund's platform team buys it, about six thousand dollars per fund per year against an admin bill of eighteen to sixty thousand, and brings it to every portfolio company at the first board meeting, so the reporting framework is set from day one. Each reporting agreement is an implementation, delivered by forward-deployed engineers. One verified founder update can go to every investor on the cap table, and the LP view is free, so LPs ask their other GPs for it. First customers come through emerging-manager communities like VCLab, and through fund admins. Why now: ILPA's reporting template version two applies to funds starting this year, and the 2025 valuation guidelines ask for traceable marks. Every fund reports every quarter, so a tool that writes and proves that report gets used every quarter.
